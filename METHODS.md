# Methods

## Measurement systems

PIRLS, TIMSS and PISA are treated as separate instruments and scales. Cross-phase comparisons concern patterns, ranks, proportions, gaps and sorting measures under explicitly documented panels; raw score points are not subtracted across surveys.

## Estimation

The production code uses the survey-specific student weights, all required plausible values, and the appropriate replicate/JRR variance design. Historical PIRLS 2001/2006/2011 core uncertainty uses the cycle-specific one-replicate-per-zone JRR rule; later cycles use the documented cycle design.

## Groups

National SES groups and international ESCS groups answer different questions and remain separate. PIRLS/TIMSS book-based background measures are not treated as identical to PISA ESCS. Gender variables are used only when source labels support a clean mapping. Language environment and migration background remain distinct constructs.

## Panels and ranks

- fixed panels for trends;
- common panels for phase comparisons;
- widest defensible panels for current comparisons;
- domain-specific panels where coverage differs.

Ranks are descriptive context, not effect estimates. Point ranks are not presented as certain orderings.

## School and class sorting

Achievement, social-background, language and migration sorting are separate measures. Between-school and between-class measures are not interpreted as causal effects of tracking, peers, school quality or policy. The all-target and modal-ISCED PISA variants are both preserved where the approval trail did not support silently discarding one specification.

## Claim discipline

Differences in statistical significance are not treated as statistically significant differences. Survey sampling intervals do not automatically cover non-response, exclusions, linking uncertainty or missing-background bias.
